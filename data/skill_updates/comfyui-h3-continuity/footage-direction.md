# Strange Footages — direction learned from approved renders

Read this for the user's fictional Strange Footages series, not for general animation or every surveillance request. New instructions override these preferences.

## Series contract

- Short, usually one uninterrupted 8-10 second take, SFW, unattended when that choice remains in force. Confirm dimensions per new batch; the current series uses vertical 9:16, 576x1024 only after confirmation.
- Make **one concrete unsettling event** visible for several seconds at normal playback. Ordinary weather, a vague dark patch, an unchanged empty yard or a fleeting unrecognizable speck is not enough. Disturbing but restrained; avoid stacking multiple supernatural events or showing a polished monster to camera.
- Keep a plausible everyday Brazilian setting, cheap capture optics and location-appropriate vegetation. Lorena/SP is context for its requested scenes, not a label to draw on screen or a setting required for all episodes.
- Use only plausible microphone ambience: air, insects, distant traffic, low hiss and subtle real contact sounds. No voice, narration, score, scare sting, growl, roar, scream or invented audible UFO engine unless explicitly requested.
- Avoid autogenerated text, clocks, watermarks and interfaces in the prompt. Generated source may still contain them; inspect the render before adding post-production text.

## Choose capture mode from the watched scene

**Installed CCTV / doorbell:** a physical fixed viewpoint observing a doorway, ground, yard, street or corridor; actual floor and architecture anchor the shot. Mild cheap-lens distortion and modest compression. No pan, zoom, subject tracking or cinematic reveal. Doorbell video does not require a bell ring or knocking.

**Trail camera:** fixed on the path, stream, clearing and vegetation, with meaningful ground visible. In night infrared, both subject and environment are grayscale under the same light falloff. Trees and foliage can partially occlude a real body.

**Sky:** if the composition looks only/mainly at the sky, use a person holding a phone. Continuous small irregular wrist tremor, breathing sway, tiny roll, imperfect delayed reframing; sky, roofs and foliage move coherently with the hand. Do not shake only the object, freeze the landscape, use a tripod/gimbal look or exaggerate shaking. Fixed CCTV can incidentally capture an aerial event when its real ground-level watched area remains substantial. The positive mixed-ground/sky examples are 49/50; sky-only 48 needs handheld treatment.

## UFOs and manual zoom

Episode 05 is a positive reference for a distant craft-like event; later handheld UFOs are approved for believable capture. Inspect available renders rather than inferring their appearance from a title.

- One distant object with restrained intermittent points of light, occasional weak red/amber/white flashes. Light spacing belongs to a rigid object, unless another behavior was requested. Optical bloom is small and limited by a cheap sensor; avoid synchronized theatrical strobes.
- A hull, when visible, is a weak uneven gray volume lost partly to distance haze and noise. Do not draw a sharp black triangle, neon border or perfectly readable sci-fi ship. Use realistic occlusion behind existing roofs, hills, trees or clouds; no dramatic beam, portal or explosion.
- Motion should supply the oddness: slow near-vertical movement, rigid lights changing orientation, hovering or descending beyond a real ridge. Keep it legible without making the craft gigantic or approaching the lens.
- In **some** clips, one hesitant manual digital zoom around seconds 3-5 from 1x to roughly 2-3x is useful. It is a camera adjustment during the same subject action. Landscape, light separation, noise and blur enlarge together; outer anchors naturally crop out. Tremor increases, a small recenter/focus hunt occurs, and detail stays limited. No dolly, sudden close-up, perfect new hull detail, automatic zoom in every clip or repeated zoom pumping.

## Bodies must have material and volume

The failures in 42/44 and 59/60 show that saying "realistic" or adding "no cartoon" at the end cannot rescue a prompt that positively asks for a featureless silhouette. Remove conflicting positive instructions such as "featureless black body", "simple silhouette", "no skin detail" or "shadow hand" when the goal is a physical creature.

Describe what should exist: rounded torso/limb thickness, continuous recognizable joints, intact skin folds, patchy coarse fur or fabric, subtle mid-gray infrared shading, weight-bearing feet/hands, contact shadows and the same softness/noise as the surroundings. An obscured face can remain unreadable without turning the entire body into black fill.

Keep unusual proportions stable **from the start**. One long torso, unusual neck orientation, tall stooped body or low gait can be unsettling; do not grow limbs, melt a face or add joints mid-shot. Avoid oversized/glowing eyes, huge teeth, costumes, clean vector edges, rubber tubes, paper cutouts, glossy CGI and posed close-ups. Optical shadows are reduced light on physical surfaces, not independently animated opaque figures.

Positive human references: 51 (head orientation inconsistent with the torso while retaining real volume) and 74 (very tall stooped body with ordinary work clothes and grounded steps). Use their successful principles, not identical scripts for every episode. Review actual skin/clothing/light and avoid repeating the same neck turn across a whole batch.

For pterosaur-like footage: a distant **physical animal**, partial wing/body glimpse in the scene, no center-frame full wingspread, landing/posing, call or cinematic approach. The earlier v3 was closer for scale and imperfect capture. Keep the dim environment and cheap quality.

If T2VA repeatedly makes a body flat, inspect representative frames and create/edit a **single in-scene plate**, preserving the approved setting and changing the body material. Save the plate and exact edit prompt in the project, verify it visually, then use it as `first_frame` on NEW/I2VA. Never feed a contact sheet as the scene plate. 59/60 were corrected this way; the resulting video still needs render review. Do not generate plates for every independent clip without a concrete reason.

## Clock and batch metadata

`category: "footage"`, `cctv_timestamp_enabled: false`. Choose a **fictional** local `cctv_timestamp_start` compatible with the stated lighting, and repeat it in the Markdown header. This records optional manual-overlay settings; it is not evidence that an event occurred.

No automatic post-production timestamp. Add one only when the user requests/clicks the manual action, after checking whether the source already has one. Our manual clock should be low-resolution, softened/pixelated and blended to match the footage, not crisp subtitle text. If two clocks appear and the user chooses the generated one (as in episode 01), remove only our overlay and retain the source. Preserve the original master.

For a mixed batch, vary the location, anomaly, anatomy/posture and capture mode, not only the title. Maintain one readable event per clip; do not dilute the batch with normal walking or indistinct fog. Use [promptera-inbox.md](promptera-inbox.md) for approval and one-job-per-project verification.
